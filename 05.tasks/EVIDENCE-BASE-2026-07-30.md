# Standards evidence base

Compiled 2026-07-30. Every document below was retrieved as PDF, text-extracted, and searched
in full for `invasive|exotic|non-native|nonnative|alien species|weed`. Hit counts are
exhaustive per document, not samples. Quotations are verbatim from the extracted text.

## Method note and one access limitation worth recording

`registry.verra.org` is closed to programmatic access. Every path and method tried returned an
identical JavaScript shell, including the UI API search endpoints, project detail pages and the
project document viewer. No Verra registry page and no Verra-hosted project description was
retrieved directly.

The Berkeley Carbon Trading Project Voluntary Registry Offsets Database downloads cleanly but
its `Project Description` column is populated only for Gold Standard and Isometric projects and
is **empty for all 4,960 VCS projects**, as well as for ACR, CAR and ART. It supports project
name search only for Verra. Anyone planning a registry-wide text analysis of VCS project
descriptions should know this before starting.

## The load-bearing quotations

**VM0033, section 4, applicability condition 2, eligible project activities, item (f):**

> Improving management practice(s) (e.g., removing invasive species, reduced grazing)

Identical in v1.0 of 20 November 2015. No species are named anywhere in VM0033; Phragmites,
Typha, Melaleuca and Spartina all return zero hits in both versions.

**VM0047 v1.0, section 8.2.1.1**, the clause that made deliberate clearing close to
disqualifying:

> Where the slope of a linear regression of stocking index values from time t = -10 to t=0,
> including site preparation, is significant and negative, clearing of pre-existing biomass is
> indicated. In this case, the project proponent must demonstrate that the clearing did not
> take place to create GHG credits [...] Where it is not possible to provide such evidence,
> the project is ineligible.

A project whose core activity is clearing a woody invader satisfies none of the three escape
conditions. The clearing is intentional, done by the proponent, and motivated by carbon
finance.

**VM0047 v1.1, section 8.2.1.2**, the carve-out added 14 May 2025:

> The removal of pre-existing woody biomass is allowed as part of site preparation where all of
> the following conditions are met: i) A t = 0 estimate has been established through plot-based
> sampling or remote sensing. ii) The biomass removed from the project site is considered a
> waste product with no commercial value. iii) Removal does not involve the clearing or
> harvesting of natural forests (primary or secondary), native tree species, or commercially
> viable timber species.

Condition (ii) sits directly against the VM0044 biochar route, where the same biomass is a
saleable feedstock.

**VM0045, Appendix A1.2**, the design that dissolves the accounting problem, present verbatim
since v1.0 in October 2022:

> IFM activity implementing management of competing vegetation to release natural regeneration:
> Matched control plots, subject to business-as-usual minimal or no treatment of competing
> invasive shrubs may be expected to witness continued suppression of natural regeneration and
> lower tree growth than the project activity that reduces the extent of competing vegetation
> and releases the regenerating trees.

**CCB Standards v3.1, criterion B2**, the whole of its engagement with the subject:

> Native species are used unless otherwise justified and invasive species and genetically
> modified organisms (GMOs) are not used.

> Identify all species used by the project and show that no known invasive species are
> introduced into any area affected by the project and that the population of any invasive
> species does not increase as a result of the project.

**VCS Standard v4.7, section 3.19.29(3)**, the eligibility carve-out:

> Such evidence is not required where the ecosystem was degraded at least 10 years prior to the
> proposed project start date, or where the dominant land cover is an invasive species that is
> threatening ecosystem health as demonstrated using the Global Invasive Species Database and
> supporting documents such as evidence from peer reviewed literature or expert judgment.

**VCS Standard v5.0, Table 12**, the same route tightened into an evidentiary test requiring a
defined dominance threshold:

> Ecological studies, surveys, or peer-reviewed literature that justifies dominance: according
> to a suitable threshold, in alignment with the Global Invasive Species Database

**SD VISta Nature Framework v1.0, section 6.6.1(7)**, the one Verra instrument where control is
both mandatory and rewarded:

> implements activities that prevent the expansion and growth of alien, non-native, or invasive
> species that may threaten target species, habitats, or people.

Footnote 91 confirms that invasive abundance is a scored Condition metric, so reducing invasive
pressure raises Condition and generates Nature Credits.

## Verra's own consultation record on the accounting obstacle

From the 97-page Summary of Public Consultation for VM0047 v1.1. Varaha ClimateAG asked what
threshold applies:

> What constitutes the threshold for such clearing of pre-existing woody biomass? Given that
> some extent of existing woody biomass may inevitably be cleared during site preparation, it
> is imperative to delineate the permissible limits for such activities.

Verra's answer concedes there is none:

> Currently, the methodology does not specify a quantitative threshold (e.g., % area cleared or
> number of trees) that constitutes disqualifying "clearing." Carbon pools and GHG emissions
> sources may be deemed de minimis where it is reasonably demonstrated that the combined
> decrease in carbon stocks or increase in GHG emissions amounts to less than 5% of the total
> GHG benefit generated by the project.

Verra also states the framing that makes deliberate invasive clearing suspect: clearing within
10 years disqualifies the area where it was done "to artificially lower the baseline". The rule
was written against gaming, and a woody invader clearance is indistinguishable from gaming on
the face of the test.

Intellecap asked for shrubs to be excluded from the definition of woody biomass. Verra declined.

## The three existing escape routes, and why each fails for a woody invader

**Declare it insignificant.** VM0005 does exactly this: "The removal of herbaceous vegetation
(including climbers and vines) is deemed an insignificant emissions source". Works for vines,
not for a Melaleuca or Prosopis stand.

**Assume it happens in the baseline.** The Explanatory Statement to Australia's Human Induced
Regeneration determination is the cleanest statement of this move anywhere in the corpus:

> Emissions from the required removal of known weed species from the project area are not
> included in the project greenhouse gas assessment boundary as it is assumed that this would
> occur under the baseline conditions.

Requires a legal or customary obligation to clear, which rarely exists. Note that both relevant
Australian methods are now closed, HIR having expired on 30 September 2023, and the successor
Integrated Farm and Land Management method did not exist as law as of 30 July 2026.

**Convert it to a durable product.** VM0044 biochar. The invader's carbon leaves the land pool
and enters a durable one. The only route currently generating revenue at scale.

## Comparator standards

| Standard | Credited | Named eligible activity | Permitted | Planting restricted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plan Vivo (PV Climate) | no | no | no | yes |
| Gold Standard | no | wetlands only, as enabling practice | yes, "should" | yes, absolute |
| ACR | no | no | Mississippi Delta burning only | yes, 95% native |
| Climate Action Reserve | no | no | no | indirectly, 95% native floors |
| Article 6.4 / PACM | no | no | no | yes, via SD Tool |
| ACCU (Australia) | no | yes, in statute | yes | yes |

None of the six genera at issue in the ecological literature, Phragmites, Melaleuca, Typha,
Prosopis, Lantana or Spartina, appears as an invader to be managed in any document of any
standard reviewed.

Notable specifics. Plan Vivo's PV Climate rulebook uses the word invasive exactly once, as a
risk-screening item, and its glossary carries no definition; the 2013 legacy standard was
stricter. ACR states that "Invasive species are not permitted whatsoever" and requires
emissions from brush removal to be accounted where above de minimis, so removal is a cost and
never a credit. The CAR US Forest Protocol contains zero occurrences of any of the search terms
across 174 pages, and the Grassland Protocol's single mention ends "The Reserve does not seek to
prescribe specific land management activities." The Article 6.4 removals standard,
A6.4-STAN-METH-002, has zero occurrences of invasive, alien, exotic, non-native, native species
or biodiversity.

Two projects with documentary evidence that invasive plant control is a named project activity.
Zero where it is the primary crediting activity.

| VCS2931 | GRASS | South Africa | VM0042 | Registered, CCB label issued |
| VCS5381 | Alien Energy Biomass Waste to Energy | South Africa | AMS-I.C. | Under development, no CCB |
| VCS5646 | Mkomazi Alienfuel Avoided Landfilling | South Africa | AMS-III.E. | Registration requested, no CCB |

VCS4255 is the only case where the project description itself was read. It commits to "control
of invasive grasses at land preparation" and to monitoring and physically removing invasive
species when detected. The two South African energy projects use invasive plant clearing as
upstream feedstock sourcing, not as a credited land management activity.

Near miss worth recording: VCS2321 Ola Hou on East Maui, using VM0047 and CCB v3.1, includes
removal of 712 feral ungulates and ungulate-proof fencing. That is invasive **animal** control
supporting an ARR project, and it is under development rather than certified.

No Melaleuca project and no Working for Water style project is registered under VCS or CCB. Of
151 CCB projects, none are in the United States, Hawaii or the Pacific islands.

## Trap for anyone repeating this search

CCB criterion B2 makes "invasive species" a compliance negation in a great many project
documents. The Generation Forest Group Project verification report states "It was verified that
the project does not introduce or manage invasive species"; Kulera Landscape REDD+ states "No
invasive species will be promoted by the project". Grepping project documents at scale drowns
in these. The distinction between "we will not introduce" and "we will remove" has to be made by
reading. A name scan of all 4,960 VCS projects against 32 invasive-related keywords returned
nine hits, most of them the opposite case: Argentine afforestation projects deliberately
planting exotics.
